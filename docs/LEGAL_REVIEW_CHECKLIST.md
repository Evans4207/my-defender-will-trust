# MY DEFENDER WILL & TRUST

*Legal Review Checklist — everything requiring counsel sign-off before launch*

### In plain English: what we have done, and what we are asking you to do

We have built a website that lets an ordinary person create their own will or living trust without hiring a lawyer. The software is finished and we are testing it privately. It has never taken a payment, and the public cannot find it. Nothing is sold in any state until you approve that state.

We have also done the legwork on the wording, so you are not starting from a blank page. The part of a will that matters most to a probate court is the self-proving affidavit — the sworn page that lets a will be accepted without tracking down the witnesses years later. For 34 states we have reproduced that state's own official form word for word, exactly as the state itself publishes it, and recorded the citation, the source web address and the date we read it; we can re-check at any time whether the state has since changed it. For 8 more states the law allows such an affidavit but never says what it should say, so we have written a first draft for you to correct — that wording is ours, not the law's, and it is not authoritative. For the remaining 9 jurisdictions we have deliberately written nothing, either because the official text is not obtainable to us or because the state may not offer this procedure at all. Each one is listed below with the reason.

We are not lawyers. None of this is legal advice, and none of it has been legally reviewed — it is desk research by a product team, done so that you can review a draft instead of building one. What we are asking you for is exactly that review: please tell us what is wrong, what is missing, and which states we may switch on. Anywhere the product shows \[ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED\], it is waiting on you. Until you sign off, every state stays marked “not approved” and nothing goes on sale.

Working state by state? Open the companion index: [**STATE\_REVIEW\_INDEX.md**](https://github.com/Evans4207/my-defender-will-trust/blob/main/docs/STATE_REVIEW_INDEX.md). It lists all 51 jurisdictions with a live link straight to each state’s own official statute, the text we captured, the date we read it, and the wording we produced — so you can open a state and see everything about it in three clicks. Every file reference in this document is also a link into the code.

This checklist covers a self-help legal document platform that produces a state-compliant Last Will & Testament or Revocable Living Trust, each with a durable financial power of attorney, a healthcare directive and a HIPAA authorisation. It is functionally complete and in internal testing. It has not processed a live payment and is not publicly indexed. Nothing ships until the items below are cleared.

Every generated document currently carries placeholder clause text marked \[ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED\]. All 51 seeded jurisdictions carry needs\_review = true and are not approved for use. These are deliberate safeguards, not oversights.

**How to use this document.** Items are grouped A to H. Section A blocks all revenue. Section B blocks each state individually. Sections C, D and H carry the highest regulatory exposure. Where an item names a file, that is where the text or logic lives in the codebase and where approved language will be placed.

*Prepared for outside counsel by the product team. The underlying build specification and per-state research notes can be supplied in full on request.*

## F Access, fiduciaries and death

Who can reach a document, and when. This is the area with the least precedent, because an estate plan is reached for at exactly the moment its owner cannot authorise access.

**Note for counsel.** Under the Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act, adopted in some form by more than 40 states, an online tool designation made inside a platform ranks ABOVE instructions in a will for access to that account. A designation made in this product would therefore outrank the customer’s own will — both an opportunity and a conflict risk.
